\section{Introduction}
\subsection{Application setup}

This work proposes a controlled numerical investigation of sparse
linear systems arising from the semi-implicit discretization of the
two-dimensional linearized shallow water equations. The objective
is not to develop a full oceanographic simulation, but to use this
model as a test case for studying the conditioning of linear systems
and the convergence of iterative solvers.

A semi-implicit finite-difference formulation will be adopted,
following the approaches discussed by Casulli (1990) and
Bonaventura and Rosatti (2002). Such formulations lead to sparse
linear systems that must be solved at each time step.

The experiments will consider a rectangular computational domain,
starting with a constant reference water depth. A second set of
experiments will introduce controlled spatial variations in the
depth coefficient. The investigation will examine how grid refinement
and increasing coefficient contrast affect the resulting matrix,
the number of iterations required for convergence, and the
computational cost of the solution process.

As a methodological extension, the work will compare the Conjugate
Gradient method without preconditioning with preconditioned variants
using Jacobi and incomplete Cholesky preconditioners. The applicability
of Conjugate Gradient will first be verified by checking the symmetry
and positive definiteness of the discrete system. This comparison
is motivated by the established literature on iterative methods for
sparse linear systems and preconditioning techniques
\cite{saad2003,benzi2002}.

The evaluation will consider iteration counts, computational time,
relative residual norms, and estimates of the condition number,
where computationally feasible. Solver tolerances and other
experimental settings will be controlled to enable meaningful
comparisons across grid resolutions and coefficient configurations.

The main research question is how grid refinement and coefficient
heterogeneity affect iterative convergence, and to what extent
incomplete Cholesky preconditioning can improve solver performance
relative to the unpreconditioned and Jacobi-preconditioned methods.
